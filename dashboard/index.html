The question

A fulfillment network with three Midwest sites has to decide how many workers to schedule. Too few and orders ship late; too many and labor is wasted. This project answers how many, what it costs, and what happens to the parcels once they leave the building.

What we did

  • Cleaned and validated 32,634 real 2023 orders and 122,370 order lines in SQL and Python
  • Backtested 7 forecasting models on 25 past dates without letting them see the future, plus a P90 high case
  • Built a staffing simulation, stress-tested plans across 36 cases and optimized schedules with a CP-SAT solver (Google OR-Tools)
  • Simulated the truck network with real 2023 weather (NOAA) and real truck routes, and priced the cheapest rescue for each late truck
  • Tested stock policies for 4,863 products and sizes on months they never saw, and found why orders still wait
  • Routed delivery vans day by day on real roads to real census tract locations, and compared vans with a parcel carrier
  • Checked every key assumption against free public data from Census, BLS, NOAA, FRED and OpenRouteService
  • Built an AI assistant that answers questions in SQL, and raised its tested accuracy from 15 to 27 of 28 questions
  • Turned it into this site: plain-English takeaways, interactive pages, phone layout and dark mode

What we would tell a manager

Six workers with overtime and temps on call is the cheapest plan that holds service. Ten workers only pays off if low productivity or absences are likely. A better forecast will not save much, because daily demand is too noisy; flexible capacity does.

Honest limits

Orders are real; wages, productivity, the truck network and delays are modeled. Every page labels which is which, and the results are a planning answer, not proof of real savings.

Run it yourself

Every number on this site can be rebuilt from free public data in about 15 minutes. Tested on a fresh copy: every saved result came out identical.

Full steps on GitHub
  1. Set up. Clone the repository and install the Python packages (Python 3.12).
    git clone https://github.com/vmtri15/workforce-capacity-planner.git
cd workforce-capacity-planner
python3 -m venv .venv
.venv/bin/python -m pip install -r requirements.txt
  2. Get free API keys from Census, BLS, NOAA, FRED and OpenRouteService, and put them in a .env file. Keys stay on your computer; a missing key only skips its step.
  3. Download the public data in one step: the 2023 orders, retail history, labor data, census tracts, weather and roads.
    .venv/bin/python scripts/fetch_public_data.py
  4. Rebuild and rerun the database and every model in order, then open the site locally. The exact commands are in the README.

The project as a DMAIC cycle

Define, Measure, Analyze, Improve, Control: the Six Sigma steps, mapped to what this project did

  1. DefineHow many workers do three Midwest sites need to ship 95% of units on time, at the lowest cost?
  2. MeasureReplayed 32,634 real 2023 orders through 30 staffing plans and 36 stress tests.
  3. AnalyzeFound flexible capacity matters more than forecast accuracy (a perfect forecast saves about $260 per 4 weeks), and 82% of truck-caused delays sit on one lane.
  4. Improve6 workers with overtime and temps on call: $41K cheaper every 4 weeks than 10 workers, 99.7% on time.
  5. ControlThe two charts below: watch packing time and the daily late-parcel rate, and act only on signals outside the limits.

Decision guardrails

Do: use the scenario range for contingency planning, workload monitoring, and data-collection priorities.

Do not: present modeled costs as savings, infer actual scheduled staffing, or claim the service target is guaranteed.

Next evidence needed: scheduled and attended labor hours, calibrated units per productive hour, actual processing timestamps, and local wage inputs.

HOW TO READ THE MODEL

Transparent by design

The prototype separates observed demand from hypothetical operating assumptions, then tests those assumptions against a fixed service definition. It supports comparison and stress testing, not automated staffing decisions.

01

Observed demand

Retained 2023 order-creation records are aggregated by recorded date and order-size segment. Missing dates remain gaps rather than assumed zero-demand days.

02

Scenario engine

Precomputed cases vary scheduled workers, overtime, temporary coverage, productivity, absence, and action lead time. All staffing and cost inputs are hypothetical.

03

Service measure

A case meets the target when at least 95% of units are completed within two operating days. Later-period validation is shown separately from the full replay.

04

Decision rule

Compare feasible policies on service, total modeled cost, backlog, and stress behavior. A passing case is a candidate for validation, not a prescribed headcount.
